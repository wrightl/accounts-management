# Alfa competitor research

**Research date:** 28 November 2026 (refresh of the 18 September 2026 snapshot)  
**Sources:** public vendor sites, pricing pages, help docs, and UK review round-ups only (no paid trials).

Prices below are **excluding VAT** unless noted. Promotional and bank-tied offers are flagged.

## What changed since September

**Alfa**

- Published pricing: Essentials £19/month (£190/year) and Premium £29/month (£290/year), ex VAT, per organisation, 30-day free trial with no card. This lands inside the £15 to £29 hypothesis.
- Recurring invoices have a full UI, so the matrix now shows Yes.
- Invoices, quotes, orders, recurring invoices, credit notes and expenses work in foreign currencies. Books stay in sterling.
- HMRC connection, VAT tax codes on every line and fraud prevention headers are built (roadmap item 17). VAT return submission is not live yet.
- Year-end page and reworked accountant pack, including a linked Excel workbook and notes for the accountant.
- Demo company with three years of data, an assistant with MCP access, and phone apps (not yet in the public stores).

**Market**

- Xero prices from 1 September 2026 are confirmed: Simple £7, Ignite £18, Grow £39, Comprehensive £55, Ultimate £70. Xero is pushing its AI agent JAX, including integrations with Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot (Xerocon London, July 2026).
- FreeAgent is unchanged at £19 sole trader and £33 limited company, with 50% introductory offers for six months. Its October 2026 spotlight added bulk imports for invoices, bills, transactions and contacts to make switching easier.
- Starling Accounting Plus is still £7/month until April 2027, then £14. Starling's March 2026 terms describe £14 as inclusive of VAT, which conflicts with reviews that quote £7 plus VAT.
- Crunch limited company Pro now reads about £90 to £94/month, Premium about £137 to £145, with a 20% six-month discount (code CRUNCH2026).
- Bonsai: Essentials $25/user ($19 annual), Premium $39 ($29 annual), Elite $59 ($49 annual, 3-user minimum).
- Harvest Flex usage fees are now live on renewals: each meter over its allowance (7 projects, 5 tasks, 5 clients, $2,000 invoiced) adds $20 to $960/month. Harvest's own help example shows a $44 bill becoming $404.

## Frame

Alfa is day-to-day books for UK consultancies, studios and small agencies: quotes, orders, milestone invoices, expenses, bank CSV matching and an accountant pack. It replaces spreadsheets and is **not** a Xero-class suite.

The real competitive fight is:

1. **Status quo** (spreadsheets plus accountant)
2. **FreeAgent** (closest ICP)
3. **Xero** (accountant default)
4. **Bonsai / Harvest** (project billing polish)

## Competitor snapshots

### Xero: accounting suite

UK default for limited companies, and the comparison even when you say you are not this.

- **ICP:** growing SMEs and accountants. Quotes, invoices, bank feeds, Hubdoc capture, VAT/MTD, payroll by plan. Projects on Ultimate. Ignite caps you at 20 invoices and 10 bills a month.
- **Entry:** Simple £7 · Ignite £18 · Grow £39 · Comprehensive £55 · Ultimate £70 (from 1 Sep 2026).
- **Since September:** AI agent JAX is the headline story, with Claude and Copilot integrations.

### FreeAgent: closest ICP

Tax-led books for freelancers and micro companies.

- **ICP:** estimates, invoices, expenses, Smart Capture, projects, time, payroll, MTD VAT and ITSA, SA and CT filing. The NatWest Group free tier is a hard price floor.
- **Entry:** Sole trader £19 · Ltd £33/mo (50% off for six months is common). Free with NatWest, RBS, Ulster or Mettle. Smart Capture Unlimited £5/mo extra.
- **Since September:** bulk import for switching, deeper landlord and CIS workflows, more practice controls.

### Starling Accounting (ex Ember): bank-tied

Closest "not Xero" messaging, now locked to Starling.

- In-app bookkeeping, invoicing, MTD ITSA, view-only accountant access. VAT via Plus. CT filing still external. Weak for multi-bank consultancies and project milestones.
- **Entry:** Essentials free · Plus £7/mo until Apr 2027, then £14 (VAT treatment of the price is inconsistent across Starling's own documents).

### Crunch: bundled service

Software plus named accountants. Tests "keep your accountant".

- Free invoicing and expenses tier; paid packages add MTD filing and human support. The buyer is buying a compliance service, not founder ops software.
- **Entry:** Free software · Ltd Pro about £90 to £94/mo · Premium about £137 to £145/mo, with 20% off for six months.

### Bonsai: client workflow

Proposals, contracts and invoices for freelancers and small agencies.

- Polished quote-to-cash, client portal, e-sign, retainers. Not UK books; syncs to Xero or QBO on higher tiers. Zoom-owned since December 2025, so roadmap risk.
- **Entry:** Essentials $25/user/mo (about £19), $19 annual. Basic $15 has no invoicing. Premium $39 ($29 annual).

### Harvest: time and invoicing

Studio staple: track time, raise invoice, sync to Xero.

- Strong time, budgets and invoicing; weak as a ledger. Flex usage fees now hit on renewal and can multiply a small team's bill, which is a clear opening for fixed per-organisation pricing.
- **Entry:** Free (1 seat, 2 projects) · Teams from $9/seat/mo annual ($11 monthly) plus usage.

### Status quo: spreadsheets plus accountant

Excel or Sheets plus Drive, bank CSV and a year-end dump. Software cost is about £0; the real cost is founder evenings and accountant clean-up. **This is what Alfa replaces.** Messaging that only attacks Xero misses the buyer who never opened Xero.

### Pricing read

The published price validates the September hypothesis. Essentials at £19 matches FreeAgent's sole trader price while undercutting FreeAgent Ltd (£33) and Xero Grow (£39) for limited companies, which is where Alfa's dividends, board minutes and director's loans earn their keep. Premium at £29 is still under both. Against Bonsai and Harvest, per-organisation pricing wins as soon as a team has two or more people. The NatWest Group free tier for FreeAgent remains the hardest objection.

## Messaging

### Their lines

- **Xero:** "Plans built for you to grow", accountant ecosystem gravity, and now AI agent JAX.
- **FreeAgent:** "Confidence in your numbers", MTD done, tax timeline.
- **Starling:** "Spend, track, relax" / "life's too short for any more logins."
- **Crunch:** software plus real accountants; peace of mind.
- **Bonsai:** proposals, contracts, get paid; client workflow first.
- **Harvest:** time to invoice to accounting sync.

### Alfa lines to keep or sharpen

- "Your books, in one place." Still the right category claim against Drive chaos.
- "Fifteen minutes a week beats a month of catch-up." Attacks spreadsheets, not Xero.
- Name the workflow: **quote, order, milestone invoice**. Nobody else owns that sentence for UK consultancies.
- "One price for the whole firm." Lean on Harvest's Flex fees and Bonsai's per-seat pricing.
- Keep "not a replacement for your accountant". Crunch owns the opposite.
- Do not lead with AI. Xero has the budget for that story; Alfa should show the assistant answering founder questions instead of claiming it.

## GTM notes

### SEO reality

"Xero vs FreeAgent" and "best accounting software sole traders UK" are owned by accountants' blogs and the big two. Head-on category SEO is expensive. The site now has comparison pages, use-case pages and an llms.txt for AI assistants. Keep leaning on consultancy, studio and agency **project billing plus books** language.

### Channels

Xero and FreeAgent win through accountant recommendation. Crunch sells the accountant. Starling, Tide and Coconut sell through the bank. Bonsai and Harvest sell through freelance and agency communities. Alfa should lead with **founders**, with accountants as invitees, not as the main acquisition channel yet.

### Watch list

- QuickBooks UK: Simple Start about £16, Essentials about £38; heavy first-six-months discounts.
- Coconut: sole-trader MTD app, £49.99/year MTD Bridging, £99.99/year Bookkeeping.
- Countingup / Tide: bank plus light books.
- FreshBooks: invoicing-led freelancers.
- Sage: established firms, payroll skew.

## Where Alfa wins and where they win

### Genuinely different

- Quote versioning, online accept, order with payment milestones.
- Per-founder inbound receipt email plus OCR.
- Reimbursement runs for co-founders.
- Multi-company read-only accountant access plus a one-click pack (CSVs, PDFs, receipts, linked workbook).
- Flat per-organisation price with no seats or usage fees.
- Honest scope: GBP books, multi-bank CSV matching, VAT codes and export, foreign-currency documents.

### Market still picks them when...

- VAT registered and need MTD filing in the product today (Xero, FreeAgent, Starling Plus). Alfa is closing this.
- The accountant insists on Xero.
- They want live Open Banking feeds, not CSV.
- They need payroll, time tracking or foreign-currency bank accounts.
- A NatWest or Starling free tier makes "good enough" books cost £0.

## What to do with this

1. **Ship VAT submission and say so loudly.** It is the top reason the market still picks Xero or FreeAgent. Once live, move the matrix cell to Yes and update the comparison pages.
2. **Own the sentence: quote, order, milestone invoice.** Keep it on the homepage.
3. **Next gaps for this ICP, in order:** (a) MTD VAT submission (in build). (b) Phone apps in the public stores. (c) Live bank feeds, still later. Time tracking and e-sign stay out unless demand is proven.
4. **Use the price as a weapon.** £19 per organisation against £33 or £39 for a limited company, and against per-seat or usage billing for studios. Build a Harvest Flex comparison page.
5. **Attack spreadsheets in copy, not Xero.** The primary buyer has a messy Drive folder. Answer "my accountant uses Xero" with the invite and pack.
6. **Counter FreeAgent's switching push.** FreeAgent now offers bulk imports. Make sure Alfa's own imports and the demo company are prominent in onboarding.
7. **Treat bank-tied free tiers as a filter, not a rival.** Win Starling CSV users who want milestones and reimbursements without changing banks.
8. **Refresh the comparison pages.** `src/lib/marketing/competitors.ts` still says checked 18 September 2026; update the date and the Crunch and Harvest pricing lines.
